The Micron Overnight Edge

A viral chart claims almost all of Micron’s gains happened overnight — buy the close, sell the open, +138,330,342% since 1990. We rebuilt it from three independent datasets, extended it to September 2026, replicated the methodology, costed the proposed $1,000-a-day bot, and stress-tested every claim.

01 · The claim, replicated on clean data

Yes — Micron really did make (almost) all of it overnight

Split every day into two trades: overnight (buy at the close, sell at the next open) and intraday (buy at the open, sell at the close). By identity the two multiply to the buy-and-hold return. On clean data — opens before 1995 are provably synthetic in retail datasets, so we start there — the split is extreme and it is not a data artifact: it holds in every start-year window, in the pristine 2005+ era, and across two independent data sources that agree to a median 0.0 bps on overlap.

The chart’s own arithmetic checks out: its two numbers multiply to ≈1,106×, and MU really did return ≈1,000× from Jan 1990 (split-adjusted ≈$1.00) to the week it went viral. Two honest corrections still apply. First, the headline is a compounding illusion — it assumes you reinvest an exponentially growing pot every night for 35 years; a fixed $1,000/day earns the arithmetic version, a few hundred dollars a year — and the overnight/intraday attribution before 1995 rests on opens that vendors largely fabricated. Second, since 2018 Micron’s intraday leg has been positive (+4.9 bps/day, statistically flat): overnight still dominates 3:1, but the “day session loses everything” half of the chart is a pre-2018 story.

02 · Expand to more stocks?

It’s a semiconductor story, not a law of nature

Same decomposition, 2018–2026, 26 liquid names. The overnight tilt concentrates exactly where the academic literature says it should — high-volatility, retail-attention, news-heavy stocks, which today means the semiconductor complex. Apple, Alphabet, Meta, J&J and P&G earned their returns during the trading day (Palantir leans that way too). Apple itself was strongly overnight-tilted in 2005–2017 and flipped completely after 2018 — these regimes are not stable.

03 · The proposed bot, costed

$1,000 a day captures $389 a year — before costs

The expected gross is 252 × $1,000 × 15.4 bps. Everything hinges on execution: market-on-close (submit by 15:55 ET) and market-on-open (by 09:28 ET) orders fill at the official auction prints — the very prices in the data — so the gross is roughly capturable. What varies is what you pay to get there. UK residents don’t get IBKR Lite; on IBKR Pro the fixed $1-minimum each way is 20 bps of round-trip friction against a 15.4 bps edge, while tiered pricing (≈$0.35/side) leaves most of it. Alpaca’s auction order types sit behind a ~$30k account minimum; Robinhood UK and Trading 212 have no on-open/on-close orders at all.

04 · The live falsification

The index version already died in production

NightShares NSPY & NIWM, June 2022 → July 2023

Two ETFs implemented exactly this trade — hold the index overnight, cash by day. In 13 months NSPY returned about −6.9% while the S&P 500 rose +22%; both funds liquidated with ~$5M combined assets. The night effect didn’t just underperform its backtest — it inverted. The index-level anomaly the viral chart generalizes from has been approximately zero since 2021 (NY Fed, “The Disappearing Overnight Drift”, July 2026). What survives is the single-name, retail-attention version — the part with the fattest tails and the least capacity.

05 · Why it exists at all

Four mechanisms, none of them free money

Retail buys the open

Retail orders queue overnight and hit an illiquid opening auction; opens print high and fade over the first hour. Much of the “overnight return” is a premium paid by buyers at the open — the strategy sells into it, which is why it works best in retail-favorite names.

Berkman et al. 2012 · Lachance 2023 · Elm Wealth 2024–25

A clientele tug-of-war

Different investor types dominate the night and the day; across 14 classic anomalies, profits accrue entirely in one session, usually with the opposite sign in the other. Cross-sectional splits like semis-vs-AAPL are the norm.

Lou, Polk & Skouras 2019 (JFE)

Beta is priced at night

CAPM holds overnight and fails intraday: high-beta stocks earn their premium while the market is shut. MU is a leveraged-beta cyclical — it sits at the intersection of every known driver.

Hendershott, Livdan & Roesch 2020 (JFE) · Boyarchenko et al. 2023 (RFS)

Or… manipulation?

The viral chart is Bruce Knuteson’s own exhibit: he argues the pattern is evidence of decades-long book-marking by large quant firms. Academia prefers the retail-flow story; his hypothesis is unrefuted but unadopted. Either way, the chart’s author doesn’t claim you can harvest it cheaply.

Knuteson 2019–22 · overnightreturns.org
06 · Verdict

Fun experiment: yes. Edge worth a bot: barely, and not like this

  1. Pilot live at one share a day for the first month. That costs ~$15 of friction and produces the one number no backtest can: your realized fills vs the official auction prints. Below ~5 bps round trip the edge survives; above it you are donating. Then scale.
  2. Diversify the leg. The same tilt at the same strength exists in TSM, MRVL, AMD, AVGO, NVDA. Five names at $200/day each keeps the bps and roughly halves the single-name gap risk that defines MU (−16% nights exist).
  3. Size it as a hobby, not income. At $1k/day the honest expectation is ~$200–390/yr with −60% years on the leg. At sizes where the P&L matters, you are running a short-vol-at-the-open book with 250 CGT disposals a year — and betting a 30-year pattern that just died at index level survives in single names.
  4. Kill criteria before you start. Pre-register them: e.g. trailing-12M overnight mean < 0, or realized cost > 5 bps/round-trip, or a −25% leg drawdown → stop. Apple’s flip is what regime death looks like; decide in advance what yours looks like.
Bottom line

The screenshot is directionally true and honestly sourced — and MU’s overnight edge is still measurably alive (+15 bps/night since 2018, t = 3.3). But its headline number is a compounding illusion, the general version of the trade is dead at index level, and the $1,000/day bot nets between −$115 and +$215 a year at a UK-accessible broker while carrying −15% overnight gaps. Run it as an experiment with kill criteria if it sounds fun. Do not run it as a business.
